About CREED

A Dedicated Catalyst for Socio-Economic Self-Reliance

Centre for Rehabilitation Education Earning Development (CREED) is an absolutely non-governmental, non-political, and non-profitable voluntary organization whole-heartedly dedicated to rendering service to distressed and vulnerable humanity.

From its beginning in the Rayer Bazar slum of Hazaribagh, Dhaka in 1987, CREED has expanded its field operations to 23 Upazilas/Thanas across 15 districts and 5 divisions, fostering sustainable livelihoods, primary schooling in deep-sea islands, and women micro-entrepreneurship.

Financial Inclusion

Organizing ultra-poor women into disciplined savings groups with capital access.

Island Education

Running 5-grade formal primary school at isolated St. Martin Coral Island.

Husbandry & Skills

Poultry rearing, plant nursery, cattle breeding, and small trade vocational training.

Methodology Blueprint

The CREED 5-Step Empowerment Model

Modeled on disciplined peer-lending and intensive capacity development to turn vulnerability into self-reliance.

01

Group Formation

15–30 ultra-poor women form a democratic savings group, electing their own President, Secretary, and Cashier.

Peer Cohesion
02

13-Week Mentorship

Supervisors attend weekly meetings. 5-day human resource training on accounting, rights, and group dynamics.

Trust & Literacy
03

Feasibility & Skills

Assessing market demand and providing 7-day skills training in poultry, nursery, or trade before disbursement.

Practical Viability
04

Disbursement

Collateral-free microloan release with a 2-week grace period before weekly declining balance repayment starts.

Zero Collateral
05

Savings & Solvency

Mandatory weekly savings of ৳20+. Members graduate out of poverty with 98.71% historical repayment rate.

St. Martin Island Educational Mission

Bringing The Light of Education to St. Martin Island

Saint Martin is a fragile coral island in the deep waters of the Bay of Bengal, 32 km from Teknaf mainland. For generations, over 8,000 residents and 1,400 children lacked formal education, with 60% of children in the island’s south completely ignored.

1998 Experimental School Initiated non-formal literacy with high community participation.
2002 CIDA Partnership Constructed 2 formal school buildings offering 5 complete primary grades.

"Over 23 continuous years, this school has transformed island life so that every family and child can access primary learning."
